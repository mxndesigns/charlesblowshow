The Segment

What "Lunch Break Live" actually is

A daily appointment at noon. When the news is still forming and everyone else is waiting for the official take, you go live for ten minutes and tell people what it means — fast, unfiltered, and in your voice. It's the column delivered live, before the column exists.

The Promise

Ten minutes, one story, your read

No panel, no teleprompter dance. One story that's driving the day, explained and interpreted by you while it's still hot. Viewers leave knowing what happened and what to think about it.

The Slot

Noon, every weekday

It lives in the lunch hour on purpose — the natural mid-day check-in. People eat, you talk, they get the day's biggest story sorted before they head back in. Predictable, ritual, must-not-miss.

The Edge

Immediacy over polish

The value is speed plus judgment. Cable spends a day deciding the frame; you set the frame at noon. Being early and being right about what matters is the whole brand.

Why it works

  • Appointment habit. A fixed daily time turns occasional viewers into a returning audience — the single hardest thing to build online.
  • Low production cost, high frequency. Ten live minutes is cheap to make daily, and frequency is what algorithms and habits both reward.
  • Repurposing engine. Every live becomes 3–5 clips, a newsletter blurb, and an audio cut. One shoot, a week of feed.
  • Distinct voice in a crowded field. The market is full of "here's the news." Almost no one offers "here's my immediate, opinionated read, live, at a set time."
  • Direct relationship. Live chat, questions, and reactions build a community that belongs to you — not rented from a network.
  • Monetizable. Memberships, a paid Q&A tier, sponsorship of a named recurring slot, and a newsletter all hang off this spine.
The bet: people don't just want to know what happened at lunch — they want to know what it means before the afternoon news cycle tells them. Lunch Break Live owns that ten-minute window.
Run of Show

Anatomy of the ten minutes

A repeatable structure viewers learn to expect. Same beats every day; only the story changes. Consistency is the format.

0:00Cold open
The hook — one sentence. Open straight into the story, no throat-clearing. "Here's the one thing you need to understand about today." A standing on-screen lower-third and the same 3-second audio sting build instant recognition.
0:30The facts
What actually happened. 90 seconds of clean, sourced summary — the who/what/when, stripped of spin, so everyone's on the same page before the opinion lands.
2:00The read
Your interpretation — the core. 4–5 minutes of "here's what it means and why it matters." The part no one else delivers at noon. Bring the column-writer's framing to a live moment.
7:00The pushback
Steelman the other side. 60–90 seconds acknowledging the strongest counter-argument and answering it. This is what earns trust and separates you from a hot-take feed.
8:30Live Q&A
One or two questions from chat. Pull a sharp question from the live audience. Makes viewers feel seen and rewards showing up live versus watching the replay.
9:30The close
The takeaway + the ask. One memorable closing line (clip bait), then a single clear CTA: subscribe / join the newsletter / be here tomorrow at noon.

Recurring signatures

  • Same cold-open line family
  • Standing title card + sting
  • "The Read" name-checked daily
  • A weekly recurring rubric (see roadmap)

Minimum kit

  • One good camera + key light
  • USB or lav mic (audio > video)
  • Streaming tool (StreamYard / Ecamm / OBS)
  • Lower-third + countdown overlay

Daily prep loop

  • AI agent surfaces top story by 11:15
  • 15-min outline: facts, read, counter
  • Go live 12:00, off by 12:10
  • Clips cut & scheduled by 1:30
Go-To-Market

What the launch feels like across all three platforms

Even weight on LinkedIn, Instagram, and YouTube — but the same story told three ways. YouTube is the home of the live and the archive; Instagram is the reach and personality engine; LinkedIn is the credibility and conversation layer. One segment, three on-ramps.

The launch arc — how it should feel week to week

Phase 1 · Tease

Mystery and anticipation. "Something arrives at noon." Cryptic Reels, a countdown, a founding essay. No full reveal yet.

Phase 2 · Reveal

Name, time, promise, and trailer drop across all three at once. Pin everything. Open the sign-up. Ask for the calendar add.

Phase 3 · Launch week

Five episodes, five days. Heavy clipping, daily Stories, reply to everything. Treat week one like an event, not a routine.

Phase 4 · Sustain

Settle into the daily rhythm. Let data pick winning formats. Introduce the weekly rubric and the membership ask.

One rule that ties it together

Film once, distribute everywhere — but never cross-post identically. The same ten minutes becomes a LinkedIn argument, an Instagram moment, and a YouTube archive. Native formatting per platform Same voice everywhere Every post points back to noon

The offer

Give a reason to hand over an email beyond "newsletter." Lead with a tangible: "The Noon Note" — a 60-second written version of each day's read, in your inbox by 12:30, plus first dibs on live Q&A.

The mechanics

  • One simple landing page (name + email)
  • Link in every bio, pinned comment, and clip end-card
  • Verbal CTA in the segment's close, every day
  • Auto-welcome email that sets the noon habit

The nudges

  • Calendar-add link ("📅 Add noon to your calendar")
  • Notification asks baked into Stories
  • Weekly "if you missed it" recap email to re-activate
  • Refer-a-friend unlock for the Q&A tier

Automation

The AI research & posting agent

A six-times-daily agent that researches the topics you care about online and publishes calibrated posts at 6 AM, 9 AM, 12 PM, 3 PM, 7 PM, and 9 PM — keeping the feed alive around the clock and feeding you the noon story on a plate.

How the agent works — the loop

01
Watch

Pull from a curated source set — news APIs, RSS, X/social trends, search — filtered to your beats (politics, race, democracy, culture).

02
Rank

Score stories by relevance to your voice, momentum/recency, and audience fit. Surface the top candidate + 2 backups.

03
Draft

Generate platform-native copy in your voice from a tuned style guide, with source links and a suggested clip/visual.

04
Approve

Route to you (or an editor) via a quick mobile approval — Slack/email/text. One tap to ship, edit, or skip.

05
Publish

Post to the scheduled slot on each platform, then log engagement to retrain ranking and copy over time.

Human-in-the-loop is the default. For a voice-driven, opinion brand, a tap-to-approve gate protects your credibility. Once trust is established, low-risk slots (6 AM brief, 7 PM clip) can graduate to fully automatic.

Build roadmap (phased)

Phase 1 · Manual-assist

Agent researches + drafts; you copy-paste and post by hand. Validate that the picks and voice are good before automating anything.

Phase 2 · Approve-to-post

Add scheduling + one-tap approval. Agent drafts on the 6-slot clock; you approve from your phone; it publishes.

Phase 3 · Multi-platform

Native per-platform formatting, auto-clipping from the live, and engagement logging feeding back into ranking.

Phase 4 · Semi-autonomous

Low-risk slots run automatically within guardrails; high-stakes posts still gated. Weekly performance report tunes the model.

Guardrails worth hard-coding

Because this is an opinion brand under your name: Always cite a source No posting on breaking tragedy without human OK Voice-match check before send Daily volume cap Easy kill-switch — the agent amplifies your judgment; it never replaces it.
