The Segment

What "The Evening Show" actually is

A nightly appointment to make sense of the day. By evening the news has fully landed — the spin has had its run, the facts have firmed up. You step in for ten minutes with the definitive read: here's what today actually meant. The last word before people put the day down.

The Promise

Ten minutes, one story, your read

No panel, no filler. The single story that defined the day, interpreted in your voice. Viewers go to bed knowing what happened and what to make of it.

The Slot

7:00 PM, every weeknight

It owns the post-dinner wind-down — the moment people finally have a second to process the day. Primetime attention, appointment ritual, the night's must-see ten minutes.

The Edge

The day in full view

Unlike a morning hot take, the evening read has all the facts in. The value is judgment delivered when the picture is complete — clarity, not just speed.

Why it works

  • Primetime habit. A fixed 7 PM slot lands in the highest-attention window of the day and turns viewers into a nightly returning audience.
  • The complete picture. Evening means the story is fully formed — you can give the authoritative read, not a guess that ages badly by dinner.
  • Repurposing engine. Each live becomes 3–5 clips, a newsletter, and an audio cut — and evening clips ride the strong night-time engagement wave.
  • Distinct in a crowded field. Plenty of evening news recaps exist; almost none offer one person's sharp, opinionated read, live, at a set time.
  • Direct relationship. Live chat and Q&A build a nightly community that belongs to you, not rented from a network.
  • Monetizable. Memberships, a paid Q&A tier, a named-slot sponsorship, and a newsletter all hang off this spine.
The bet: at the end of the day people don't just want a recap — they want someone they trust to tell them what it all meant. The Evening Show owns that final ten-minute window before the day closes.
Run of Show

Anatomy of the ten minutes

A repeatable structure viewers learn to expect. Same beats every night; only the story changes. Consistency is the format.

0:00Cold open
The hook — one sentence. Open straight into the story of the day, no throat-clearing. "Of everything that happened today, here's the one thing that matters." Standing lower-third + a 3-second sting build instant recognition.
0:30The day in brief
What actually happened. 90 seconds of clean, sourced summary — now with the full day's facts settled, so everyone shares the same picture before the read.
2:00The read
Your interpretation — the core. 4–5 minutes of "here's what it meant and why it matters." The authoritative end-of-day take no one else delivers at 7 PM.
7:00The pushback
Steelman the other side. 60–90 seconds on the strongest counter-argument, answered. This earns trust and separates you from a hot-take feed.
8:30Live Q&A
One or two questions from chat. Pull a sharp question from the live audience — rewards showing up live versus catching the replay.
9:30The close
The takeaway + the ask. One memorable closing line (clip bait), then a single clear CTA: subscribe / join the newsletter / be here tomorrow at 7.

Recurring signatures

  • Same cold-open line family
  • Standing title card + sting
  • "The Read" name-checked nightly
  • A weekly recurring rubric (see roadmap)

Minimum kit

  • One good camera + key light
  • USB or lav mic (audio > video)
  • Streaming tool (StreamYard / Ecamm / OBS)
  • Lower-third + countdown overlay

Daily prep loop

  • AI agent surfaces top story by 6:00 PM
  • 30-min outline: facts, read, counter
  • Go live 7:00, off by 7:10
  • Clips cut & scheduled by 8:30

The launch arc — how it should feel week to week

Phase 1 · Tease

Mystery and anticipation. "Something arrives at 7." Cryptic Reels, a countdown, a founding essay. No full reveal yet.

Phase 2 · Reveal

Name, time, promise, and trailer drop across all three at once. Pin everything. Open sign-up. Ask for the calendar add.

Phase 3 · Launch week

Five nights, five episodes. Heavy clipping, nightly Stories, reply to everything. Treat week one as an event.

Phase 4 · Sustain

Settle into the nightly rhythm. Let data pick winning formats. Introduce the weekly rubric and the membership ask.

One rule that ties it together

Film once, distribute everywhere — but never cross-post identically. The same ten minutes becomes a LinkedIn argument, an Instagram moment, and a YouTube archive. Native formatting per platform Same voice everywhere Every post points back to 7 PM

Automation

The AI research & posting agent

A six-times-daily agent that researches the topics you care about online and publishes calibrated posts at 6 AM, 9 AM, 12 PM, 3 PM, 7 PM, and 9 PM — keeping the feed alive all day and building toward the 7 PM live.

How the agent works — the loop

01
Watch

Pull from a curated source set — news APIs, RSS, X/social trends, search — filtered to your beats (politics, race, democracy, culture).

02
Rank

Score stories by relevance to your voice, momentum/recency, and audience fit. Surface the top candidate + 2 backups for the night.

03
Draft

Generate platform-native copy in your voice from a tuned style guide, with source links and a suggested clip/visual.

04
Approve

Route to you (or an editor) via quick mobile approval — Slack/email/text. One tap to ship, edit, or skip.

05
Publish

Post to the scheduled slot on each platform, then log engagement to retrain ranking and copy over time.

Human-in-the-loop is the default. For a voice-driven opinion brand, a tap-to-approve gate protects your credibility. Once trust is established, low-risk slots (6 AM brief, 9 PM clip) can graduate to fully automatic.

Build roadmap (phased)

Phase 1 · Manual-assist

Agent researches + drafts; you copy-paste and post by hand. Validate the picks and voice before automating anything.

Phase 2 · Approve-to-post

Add scheduling + one-tap approval. Agent drafts on the 6-slot clock; you approve from your phone; it publishes.

Phase 3 · Multi-platform

Native per-platform formatting, auto-clipping from the live, engagement logging feeding back into ranking.

Phase 4 · Semi-autonomous

Low-risk slots run automatically within guardrails; high-stakes posts still gated. Weekly report tunes the model.

Guardrails worth hard-coding

Because this is an opinion brand under your name: Always cite a source No posting on breaking tragedy without human OK Voice-match check before send Daily volume cap Easy kill-switch — the agent amplifies your judgment; it never replaces it.
