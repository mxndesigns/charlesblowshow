# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Politically engaged adults who want to *understand* the day, not just feel angry about it — readers of opinion columns, NPR/PBS listeners, Substack subscribers, YouTube news watchers. They arrive on a phone between things (lunch, commute, evening wind-down) and on desktop when they sit to read. The job to be done: "make sense of race, politics, and power today, from someone I trust, and leave me equipped instead of anxious."

## Product Purpose

The Charles Blow Show is a daily independent political-media product — two segments a day (☀️ *Lunch Break Live*, a 10-min live take; 🌙 *The Evening Show*, a 20-min guest conversation) plus a companion site and newsletter. It exists to be a literate, principled alternative to cable-news noise and algorithmic outrage. Success ladder: 10k YouTube subscribers → sustained audience growth → profitability. The brand surface (site, thumbnails, lower-thirds, social) IS the product's first impression and trust signal.

## Brand Personality

**Incisive · Principled · Warm · Unflinching · Literate.** Voice is plainspoken and precise — the smart friend at the table, not a lecturer. Confident enough to say the quiet part; warm enough to leave people equipped, ending on "What now?" rather than dread. Visually the new identity leads with a **bold, broadcast-grade wordmark** (heavy condensed sans, stacked, with a bracket device) — a strong on-air signature that telegraphs "a real daily show," while the writing stays editorial and literate.

## Anti-references

- **Cable-news loud** — no red/white/blue chrome, no lower-third clutter, no breaking-news bombast (CNN/Fox graphics language).
- **Cold corporate / SaaS** — no sterile geometric-sans neutrality, no startup-minimalism beige.
- **Partisan campaign** — no flag motifs, stars-and-stripes, or slogan-badge campaign-logo energy.
- **Loud podcast-bro** — no neon-on-black, mic icons, grunge, or streamer-aggression.

Net: serious independent newsroom with the confidence of a flagship show — bold, not shouty; warm, not cozy; principled, not partisan.

## Design Principles

1. **Authority through restraint.** One bold structural idea (the wordmark + bracket) and one warm accent (ember) carry the brand. Impact comes from weight and composition, not decoration.
2. **Equip, don't agitate.** Every surface should leave the visitor clearer and calmer, mirroring the show's "What now?" ethos. No outrage-bait visual grammar.
3. **Show up daily and recognizable.** The mark must read instantly at thumbnail, avatar, and lower-third scale — built for a daily cadence across YouTube, social, and the site.
4. **Literate, not loud.** Bold display voice paired with genuinely readable long-form. The shout is the logo; the conversation is the prose.
5. **Independent, not institutional.** Distinct from both cable news and campaign politics — a person's name on the masthead, owned and accountable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**: body text ≥ 4.5:1, large/bold text ≥ 3:1. Ink-on-paper passes; ember reserved for accents and large text, never small body copy.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any entrance/live animations (crossfade or instant fallback).
- Don't encode meaning in color alone (e.g. the "live" state pairs the ember dot with a "LIVE" label).
- Captions/transcripts assumed for video; alt text written in-voice, not generic.
