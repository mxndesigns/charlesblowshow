Guest & Audience Outreach Playbook

Two tracks: booking guests for The Evening Show, and recruiting founding members. Companion to the outreach tracker.

Track A

Guest booking — The Evening Show

1. the one-line pitch.

Drop this exact line into every invite — one sentence, no jargon:

“A daily independent political show — a real evening conversation about race, politics, and power, not a cable hit. I'd love you on The Evening Show.”

Pick one ask to close with, depending on how warm the relationship is:

  • Warm / you know them: “Would you come on The Evening Show?”
  • Cold-ish / curious: “Can I send you the show and a few dates?”
  • Either: “Want a two-minute look at what the conversation would be?”

2. anatomy of a good opener (3–5 sentences).

Every message has the same four parts. Keep it under five sentences — long notes get skipped.

  • Specific hook. Reference something real — their recent book, piece, ruling, or remark. This proves it isn't a blast.
  • The pitch. The one line above, verbatim.
  • Why them. One short reason they fit (“you've lived this argument,” “you say it plainly”).
  • The ask. One clear question. Make it easy to say yes.

4. daily send workflow.

  • Pace it: 5–10 invites a day across email, DM, and intros — few enough that you can reply to everyone same-day.
  • Before sending, log the name, contact method, the angle, and the date in the tracker. Set Status = Invited.
  • Log every reply the same day. Mark Response as yes / no / no-reply and update Status.
  • No reply after ~5–7 days: one soft nudge is fine (“no pressure — happy to send dates whenever”), then leave it.
  • Don't blast. If you can't personalize the hook, it isn't ready to send.

5. when a guest says yes.

  • Mark Response = yes and Status = Booked in the tracker; note the agreed date and topic.
  • Send the confirmation with date, run-of-show, the segment length (~20 min), and the recording link.
  • Send the guest release for signature before recording, and confirm name, title, and pronunciation.
  • Brief lightly. Share the one or two questions the conversation will open on — never a full script.

6. guest confirmation email (send manually).

A few logistics: we'll record at [link / location], and I'll send a short guest release to sign beforehand. If you can reply with your preferred name, title, and the pronunciation, I'll make sure we get it right on air. Anything you'd like me to know going in, just tell me here.

Fill before sending [Name], date, time, recording link, and the opening topic.

7. quick do's and don'ts.

  • Do lead with the specific detail — the book, the piece, the ruling. It's the reason they read past line one.
  • Do promise the format honestly: an unhurried, fair conversation, not a gotcha.
  • Don't paste the same note to everyone — people in this world compare notes.
  • Don't oversell or flatter. The show and the format are the pitch.
  • Don't skip the release. Get it signed before you record.
